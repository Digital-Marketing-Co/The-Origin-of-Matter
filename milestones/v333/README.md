Scientific hierarchy:
1. Late Eurasian archaic contact — Oase 1 records a Neanderthal ancestor ~4–6 generations earlier and 6–9% Neanderthal-derived ancestry.
2. Maritime dispersal through Wallacea — Sahul was never connected to Sunda by dry land; successful settlement required sea crossings.
3. Sahul arrival — Madjedbebe anchors human occupation around 65 ka.
4. Founder demography — stochastic models estimate ~1,300–1,550 census founders for low quasi-extinction risk under modeled assumptions, potentially accumulated through repeated voyages.
5. Ecological adaptation — grinding stones, plant processing, pigments, and ground-edge technologies demonstrate broad behavioral flexibility.
6. Persistent continental occupation — distinguishes physical arrival from lasting demographic establishment.

Primary anchors: Fu et al., Nature 2015; Clarkson et al., Nature 2017; Bradshaw et al., Nature Ecology & Evolution 2019; Bird et al., Scientific Reports 2019; Florin et al., Nature Communications 2020.

Evidence firewall: Oase 1 is not treated as a direct ancestor of later Europeans; 65 ka Madjedbebe is not treated as an uncertainty-free universal colonization date; Sahul settlement is not reduced to accidental rafting; demographic-model estimates are explicitly scenario-dependent.
