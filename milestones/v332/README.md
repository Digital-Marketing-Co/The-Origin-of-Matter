## Scientific scope

This milestone extends the Homo sapiens emergence sequence through:

1. Omo-Kibish stratigraphy and Omo I minimum-age revision.
2. KHS Tuff correlation to the Shala Qi2 eruption (233 ± 22 ka).
3. Herto as a later eastern African Homo sapiens population and the importance of preserving geochronological uncertainty.
4. Misliya Cave (177–194 ka) as evidence of an early Levantine sapiens dispersal substantially preceding the principal Late-Pleistocene ancestry-forming expansion.
5. Ust'-Ishim (~45 ka) as a genome near the western/eastern Eurasian population split and a recombination-based clock for Neanderthal admixture.
6. Bacho Kiro Initial Upper Palaeolithic genomes (45,930–42,580 cal BP), including recent Neanderthal ancestors and affinities toward later East Asian/American populations.
7. Explicit separation of archaeological culture, anatomy, geography, and genome ancestry.
8. A population-dynamic model distinguishing migration from long-term genealogical persistence.

## Evidence firewall

The milestone explicitly rejects these overstatements:

- Omo I is exactly 233,000 years old.
- Herto proves a simple linear transition from archaic Homo to modern humans.
- Misliya is the single successful Out-of-Africa event ancestral to all living non-Africans.
- Ust'-Ishim can be reduced to a simple western-versus-eastern Eurasian category.
- Initial Upper Palaeolithic technology uniquely identifies one genetic population.
