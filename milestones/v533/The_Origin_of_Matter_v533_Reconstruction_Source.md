## Primary peer-reviewed anchor
Fotio Y, Al Masri S, Shi Z, Le J, Das S, Rubtsova VI, Mabou Tagne A, Jang C, Swarup V, Piomelli D. “Metabolic reallocation in spinal cord oligodendrocytes drives chronic pain in mice through neuronal Aβ42 production.” Science Translational Medicine. 2026;18(865):eaea8738. DOI: 10.1126/scitranslmed.aea8738. Published September 2, 2026.

## Core hierarchy
Peripheral injury → oligodendrocyte NAAA-linked metabolic reallocation → myelin-protein synthesis down + lipid-biosynthesis program up but rerouted → altered myelin composition/integrity → axonal dysfunction → neuronal APP accumulation → BACE1 increase → Aβ42 production during a critical window → persistent pain-related behavior.

## Causal interventions
BACE1 inhibition and intrathecal 4G8 intervention prevent pain chronification in the formalin mouse model. Oligodendrocyte-specific NAAA deletion prevents Aβ42 production and chronic pain after formalin injury and sciatic nerve ligation.

## Evidence boundary
Principal causal evidence is in mice. The study does not establish Alzheimer’s disease as the cause of chronic pain and does not establish anti-amyloid or NAAA-targeted therapy for human chronic pain.
