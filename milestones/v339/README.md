## Scientific scope
1. First major South-American dispersal: early Southern Native American ancestry.
2. Second Holocene dispersal through Central America into South America with partial replacement and long-term continuity.
3. Third Late-Holocene dispersal linking northeast South America and Ceramic-period Caribbean ancestry beginning at least ~1.3 ka ago.
4. Southern-Andean agricultural transition in the Uspallata Valley: local adoption of farming before later migration.
5. Fine-scale Amazonian and lowland population structure and the limitations of using highly drifted Surui/Karitiana as continental proxies.
6. Widespread natural-selection signals affecting immunity, metabolism, fertility and development.
7. Adaptive archaic introgression, especially enrichment in epidermal/keratin-related pathways.
8. Colonial-era bottlenecks, long runs of homozygosity, and recent effective-population-size collapse superimposed on older Indigenous diversity.
9. Structural variation as a major unresolved layer for Indigenous-American population genomics.

## Primary-source anchors
- Castro e Silva et al., *Nature* 653 (2026) 134-145 — 128 high-coverage Indigenous American genomes; at least three South-American dispersals; natural selection; archaic introgression; colonial bottlenecks; structural-variation gap.
- 2026 *Nature* study of the Uspallata Valley — 46 ancient genomes; local adoption of agriculture and later migration around 810-700 cal BP.
- Caribbean ancient-genome work, *Nature* (2020) — >98% replacement of Archaic-related ancestry in sampled islands by Ceramic Age populations related to northeast South America.
- Schloissnig et al., *Nature* 644 (2025) 442-452 — long-read structural variation in 1,019 diverse humans; >100,000 sequence-resolved biallelic SVs.

## Evidence firewall
- Three South-American dispersals do not mean three independent migrations from Asia.
- Cultural transition does not imply genome-wide replacement.
- Surui or Karitiana should not be treated as universal proxies for all lowland South-American diversity.
- Selection scans do not by themselves identify the exact historical adaptive pressure.
- Archaic introgression can be evolutionarily important without constituting a large genome-wide ancestry fraction.
- Current high-coverage SNV datasets do not capture all structural genomic diversity.
