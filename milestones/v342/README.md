## Scientific scope

This milestone converts the long-read and Indigenous-data-sovereignty framework from v341 into an end-to-end hypothetical technical architecture for a future Indigenous-American population reference program. It is explicitly **not** authorization to sample any named Indigenous community. Participation is assumed to require community/Tribal invitation, collective governance, and individual informed consent.

### Six-level hierarchy

1. **Sovereign governance** — community authority, consent, stewardship, permitted uses, publication review, sample disposition and benefit rules precede collection.
2. **Representative sampling** — sampling is stratified across independently governed participating communities without treating “Indigenous American” as one biological population.
3. **Long-read diploid assembly** — high-fidelity/ultra-long sequencing, T2T-aware QC and de novo phased assembly preserve novel sequence, repeats and structural variants.
4. **Graph pangenome construction** — phased haplotypes become graph paths rather than deviations from a single canonical reference.
5. **Clinical and analytical calibration** — mapping error, SV sensitivity, tandem-repeat accuracy, Mendelian consistency, rare-variant false positives and VUS burden are measured across represented populations.
6. **Governed reuse and benefit** — tiered access, federated analysis, auditing, benefit delivery and community-controlled long-term stewardship complete the infrastructure.

community invitation → Tribal/community governance instrument → individual consent → collective authorization → biospecimen stewardship → balanced sampling → kinship-aware design → high-molecular-weight DNA → HiFi/ultra-long sequencing → diploid phasing → T2T quality control → de novo assembly → SV/repeat callsets → graph construction → haplotype-path provenance → clinical calibration → tiered access → federated analysis → benefit auditing → community-controlled long-term stewardship

## Evidence firewall

- This blueprint does **not** authorize sampling any Indigenous community.
- Open access is **not** assumed to be the default for Indigenous genomic data.
- A continental Indigenous pangenome must not encode political/cultural identity as if it were a biological taxonomy.
- Federated learning can reduce raw-data movement but does **not** eliminate privacy risk.
- A long-read pangenome does not automatically improve clinical care; error reduction must be demonstrated empirically.
- The sample-allocation equation in the atlas is conceptual, not a prescriptive quota.

## Primary-source anchors

- Global Indigenous Data Alliance — **CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance**: Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility and Ethics.
- NIH `NOT-OD-22-214` — responsible management and sharing of American Indian/Alaska Native participant data, with explicit recognition of Tribal sovereignty, laws, policies and preferences.
- Native BioData Consortium — Indigenous-led sovereign genomic biorepository precedent.
- Liao et al., *Nature* 617 (2023) 312–324 — draft human pangenome from 47 phased diploid assemblies; 34% fewer small-variant discovery errors and 104% more structural variants detected per haplotype than GRCh38 workflows in reported benchmarks.
- Schloissnig et al., *Nature* 644 (2025) 442–452 — 1,019 long-read genomes across 26 populations, >100,000 sequence-resolved biallelic structural variants and ~300,000 multiallelic tandem-repeat loci.
- Indigenous-data-sovereignty/federated-analysis literature — compute-to-data as an option for community-consented analysis without unrestricted raw-genome export.
