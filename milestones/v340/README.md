## Six-Level Scientific Architecture

1. Environmental challenge — chronic high-altitude hypoxia imposes persistent physiological stress.
2. Temporal genetic adaptation — ancient Andean allele-frequency trajectories reveal selection acting across millennia.
3. Trans-Pacific contact — ancient Rapanui genomes record pre-European Native-American admixture.
4. Demographic resilience — genomes distinguish contact and later colonial collapse from unsupported pre-European ecocide bottlenecks.
5. Variant-class expansion — long-read sequencing exposes structural variation missing from SNP-centered Indigenous datasets.
6. Responsible population references — community governance, long reads and pangenomic representation become one integrated scientific infrastructure.

## Evidence Firewall

- Andean high-altitude adaptation is not reduced to one EGLN1 variant; the phenotype is polygenic and physiological, and EGLN1 is not consistently top-ranked in genome-wide Andean scans.
- Rapa Nui Native-American ancestry is not attributed to post-European admixture: ancient Rapanui carry the ancestry without detectable European ancestry, and the admixture is dated to about 1250–1430 CE.
- The Rapa Nui genomes date contact but do not identify whether Polynesians traveled to South America, Native Americans traveled west, or a more complex interaction occurred.
- Absence of a severe 1600s genomic bottleneck does not imply absence of ecological or social stress; it specifically rejects a strong pre-European genetic-collapse model of the proposed magnitude.
- High-coverage short-read Indigenous-American genomes do not capture all important variation; structural variants and tandem-repeat loci remain major missing classes.
- Genomic utility does not justify unrestricted data sharing; community consent, privacy, sovereignty, repatriation and benefit-sharing are integral to responsible population genomics.
